# FCV Risk Country-Sector Briefing Generator

Produce a structured analytical briefing on recent FCV (fragility, conflict, and violence) risks for a specified country and sector, and their potential impacts on active World Bank portfolio projects.

---

## Step 1: Confirm Inputs

Check the user's message for both required inputs:
- **Country** (or sub-national region)
- **Sector** (e.g. water, education, agriculture, transport, health, social protection)

If either is missing, ask for both in a single message using the ask_questions tool. Once confirmed, proceed without further clarification.

---

## Step 2: Research

Run internal and external searches in parallel where possible.

**Internal sources first:**

1. Search the **WB Internal Knowledge base** for: `[country] FCV fragility conflict risk [sector]` — to find CPFs, CLRs, ABPEs, PADs, ICRs, IEG reports, and other operational documents referencing FCV conditions.
2. Search **Projects** for: `[country] [sector] active portfolio` — to identify active lending operations, retrieve project objectives from PADs, and check ISRs and Aide Memoires for flagged implementation issues. Also look for Restructuring Documents that may indicate prior FCV-related project adjustments.

**External sources second:**

3. Use **web search** following this source preference order. Prefer higher-ranked sources; use lower-ranked ones only when higher-ranked sources are unavailable or insufficient. Also search for sector-specific conditions as relevant (WASH, IPC food security, Education Cluster, Health Cluster).

   1. **ICG / CrisisWatch** — International Crisis Group conflict analysis and monthly risk ratings
   2. **Multilateral or UN sources** — OCHA, UNHCR, WFP, UNICEF, OHCHR, World Bank, UNDP, IOM DTM, IDMC
   3. **Reputable national or regional outlets** from the country — for ground-level reporting and local context
   4. **Reputable international wires or broadcasters** — Reuters, AP, BBC, Al Jazeera, VOA

Note any source gaps explicitly in the output.

---

## Step 3: Produce the Briefing

Output the briefing in exactly four sections below. Target **650–850 words** total (excluding headers). Use bullet points throughout; avoid prose paragraphs.

At the top of the briefing, include a one-line header:

> **FCV Risk Briefing | [Country] — [Sector] Sector | [Month Year]**

If any finding warrants immediate task team attention, add a bold **⚠ Escalation Flag** line immediately below the header before Section 1.

---

### Section 1 — FCV Risk Summary (Last 3 Months)

Provide **4–6 bullets** on the most significant FCV developments. Each bullet must include:
- **What** happened (1–2 sentences)
- **Where** (region or sub-national area if available; national if not)
- **Source + approximate date** (e.g. *OCHA Situation Report, Apr 2025*)

If a specific source is unavailable, note: *[Source not located — based on [alternative or general context]]*

Cover at minimum: armed conflict or security incidents, displacement trends, governance or access constraints, and any humanitarian triggers relevant to the sector.

---

### Section 2 — Portfolio Exposure Analysis

Draw on PADs (Project Appraisal Documents) for project objectives, ISRs (Implementation Status and Results Reports) for current implementation status, Aide Memoires for recent supervision findings, and Restructuring Documents for any prior FCV-related adjustments. Reference IEG Reports and ICRs (Implementation Completion and Results Reports) for lessons from closed operations where relevant. For country-level strategic context, reference the CPF (Country Partnership Framework), CLR (Completion and Learning Review), and ABPE (Annual Bank Portfolio Exercise) where available.

For each active World Bank project in the specified sector and country, provide:

**[Project Name] — [Project ID]**
- *Objective:* One sentence (from PAD)
- *Current status:* One sentence (from most recent ISR or Aide Memoire)
- *FCV risks to implementation:* Bullet the specific risks (e.g. site access, contractor safety, supply chain disruption, community tensions, staff security, counterpart capacity)
- *Overall risk level:* **Low / Medium / High** *(with one-sentence rationale)*

If no specific project is identified through internal search, note this explicitly and describe the likely risk profile for a typical project of that type in the country context.

---

### Section 3 — Potential Impacts on Delivery

Describe **3–5 concrete impacts** the identified risks could have on project delivery, disbursement, or outcomes. For each:
- Name the impact (bold)
- Link it explicitly to a risk from Section 1 (e.g. *"Linked to: armed incidents in northern [Region]"*)
- Specify which project or project type is affected

Examples of impact types: implementation delays, cost escalation, contractor withdrawal, supervision gaps, community engagement breakdown, disbursement slowdown, fiduciary risk.

---

### Section 4 — Recommended Actions

Suggest **2–3 specific, practical actions** the task team could take within the next 30–60 days. Each action should:
- Be actionable (not generic advice)
- Name the responsible party where possible (e.g. task team leader, security focal point, government counterpart)
- Reference the specific risk it addresses

Examples: trigger the project's contingency plan, request a security assessment from the WBG Security Unit, convene a virtual supervision mission, engage the relevant UN cluster, adjust implementation modality to remote supervision, document force majeure conditions, flag in the next ISR update, or initiate a Restructuring if scope adjustments are needed.

---

**Do not fabricate sources or project details.** If data is unavailable for a specific area or time period, state this clearly: *"No recent [source type] located for [area/period]."* If a project document is not retrievable through mAI's internal search, note this rather than inferring content.
ⓘ
What changed: exact WBG documents are mentioned, sources are ranked by credibility, the output has 4 structured sections, and each impact is explicitly linked to a risk, etc.
